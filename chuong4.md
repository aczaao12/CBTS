
**4. Salting, Drying and Smoking of fish**
Salting, smoking and drying have continued as preservation techniques to the present day.

Modern developments have centred around understanding and controlling the processes to achieve the standardised product demanded by today's market.

**4.1 Salting fish**
Preserving fish by salting, drying, smoking, fermenting, acid curing or any combination of these is known as “curing”.

Solution of these salts is called “brine”.

**4.1.1 The effect of salt on preservation**
Remove water, decrease a w.

Inhibit the activity of enzyme, prevent microbial.

**4.1.3.1 Dry salting**
Salting in which fish are stacked covered in solid salt and the brine formed is allowed to flow away.

Fish are split open, gills and intestines removed, washed and salt is scrubbed into it.

A fish to salt ratio ranging from 1:3 to 1:10 depending upon the size of fish.

**4.1.3.1 Dry salting**

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|------------|---------------|
| * Better to reduce moisture content | * Loss high amount of water |
| * Fish is quickly absorbed salt | * Poor characteristic (shrunken skin) |
| * Do not need special utensils | * Easy to oxidation |
| | * Easy to spoilage in the beginning |

**Kench curing**
Split white fish opened flat and laid in layers between layers of salt, such that the liquid formed is allowed to drain away.

**4.1.3.2 Wet salting**
Salting in which fish are immersed in the strong brine (pickling), usually 36% concentration of salt for 15 to 20 minutes.

Pickling in concentrated brines for long periods is used for long term preservation of fatty fish.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|------------|---------------|
| * Less oxidation | * Need special utensils |
| * Osmotic pressure is high in the beginning | * Fish is slowly absorbed salt in the end |
| * Can adjust desirable concentration of brine | |
| * Fish skin is not too much shrunken | |

**Matjes cure**
A lighter cure using 80-90% saturated brine is used for herring to give a soft texture and characteristic flavor.

The product has a lower salt content and has to be kept chilled because it spoils readily at higher temperatures.

**Light cure (Gaspé cure)**
Split white fish is immersed in the brine for 2 to 3 days and then dried in the sun or in drying kilns.

**Heavy cure (hard cure)**
The salted and dried product is known as a heavy cure and has a long shelf life.

**4.1.3.3 Injecting salting**
Injection salting is a rapid curing method compared with brine and dry salting, and it gives substantially higher processing yields.

**4.2 Drying fish**
Removal of moisture by evaporation from fish, as a mean of curing for preservation.

Natural drying in the sun and wind, or drying over the wood fires are still used today in some parts of the world.

**4.2.1 The effect of drying on preservation**
Removal of moisture reduces a w.

Reduce the water content to about 15%, corresponding to a w 0,6 to prevent the growth of bacteria and moulds.

**4.2.2 The principle of drying**
Fish will lose water by drying only if the water vapour pressure of the surrounding air is less than the vapour pressure of the moisture in fish.

**4.2.2.1 The external diffusion of moisture**
The moisture which moves out of the surface of the fish is calculated by the equation: dW = B(E − e)F dt.

W: quantity of water moves out (kg)

B: coefficient of evaporation

E: saturated vapour pressure on the surface of fish (N/m2)

e: particularly vapour pressure of air (N/m2)

F: evaporation area (m2)

t: time of evaporation (h)

**4.2.2.2 The internal diffusion of moisture**
Water within the layers transfers to the surface layer of fish and is calculated by the equation: dW = K F (dc/dx) dt

W: quantity of water diffuses (kg)

K: coefficient of diffusion

dc/dx: gradien of the moisture (moisture difference between the internal layers)

F: diffusion area (m2)

t: time of diffusion (h)

**4.2.3.1 Air drying or contact drying**
Heat is transferred to the fish from heated air or from a heated surface to carry the moisture away.

**4.2.3.2 Vacuum drying**
By enhancement of evaporation rate by produce pressure.

The water is removed from the fish by the vacuum pump.

**4.2.3.3 Freeze drying**
Employing very low pressures to sublime ice from frozen fish.

Ice sublimes at pressures below 0.64 kPa and the water vapor is removed by the vacuum pump.

**4.3 Smoking fish**
The penetration of volatile compounds resulting from incomplete burning of wood into food products.

Wood smoke: The wood used for sawdust or chip of maple, oak, ...

The composition of smoke depends on the water content, temperature to produce the smoke.

The smoke:

Golden color of carbonyls on the flesh.

Phenol components are most important for acceptable.

**Wood smoke**
The wood used for smoke is usually sawdust or chip of wood such as maple, oak, ...

The composition and characteristics of smoke depend on the type of wood, the water content of the wood, the temperature to which it is heated to produce the smoke.

**The smoke product**
Golden color is due to the interaction of carbonyls with amino components on the flesh surface.

Phenol compounds and other components soluble in water are the most important factors impacting acceptable flavor in smoked products.

**Type of smoke**
Natural smoke

**The mechanical kiln**
Smoke is moved over fish that depends on forced convection and temperature control.

**Torry kiln:** It is discovered by Torry Research Station, Scotland and used for smoking. Torry kiln consists of a smoke chamber in which fuels are burned to produce the smoke. The equipment is provided with precipitator, fan etc. The smoke produced from the chamber pass where large particles are filtered.
